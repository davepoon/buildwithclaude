# Channel Formats: Worked Examples

One source, drafted into each channel so you can see the spec applied. These are illustrative — always apply the live brand profile and the current spec in `channels/<id>.md`.

## The source

A blog post: **"Why we killed our 40-dashboard analytics setup and shipped 3."** Core message: *fewer dashboards, better decisions.* Persona: Data Lead. Voice: confident, plain-spoken, dry.

The same idea below is re-shaped per channel — same message, different container. It is *not* copy-pasted across channels.

---

## linkedin

*Target 900–1,300 chars; hook before the ~140-char fold; 0–3 hashtags.*

```
We deleted 37 of our 40 dashboards. Nobody noticed anything was missing.

Here's what we learned building analytics people actually use:

Most dashboards exist to feel data-driven, not to change a decision. If no one can name the action a chart drives, the chart is decoration.

So we ran an audit. For each dashboard we asked one question: what decision does this change, and who makes it?

37 had no answer.

The 3 that survived each map to a real weekly decision — what to build, what to fix, what to cut. Load time went from 9 seconds to under 1. The team checks them because they matter.

Fewer dashboards, better decisions. Start by deleting the ones nobody can defend.

What's the dashboard you keep "just in case"?

#analytics #datastrategy
```

## blog

*Excerpt 150–250 words, or short post 500–800; title + meta ≤ 155 chars.*

```
Title: Why we killed 37 dashboards and kept 3
Meta: We audited every analytics dashboard we owned with one question. Only three survived. Here's the test and what changed.

## The audit that deleted 37 dashboards

We had 40 analytics dashboards. We now have 3. Nobody has asked where the other 37 went.

For years we added dashboards to feel data-driven. Most of them never changed a decision — they loaded slowly and made the analytics page look busy. So we ran one test on each: **what decision does this change, and who makes it?**

Thirty-seven had no answer. We cut them.

## The three that survived

Each remaining dashboard maps to a real weekly decision — what to build, what to fix, what to cut. The page that used to take nine seconds to load now takes under one.

Fewer dashboards, better decisions. If a chart can't name the action it drives, it's decoration — and you can delete it today.
```

---

Note how the message is constant but the *shape* is not: the thread front-loads each tweet, the short-video script marks on-screen cues, the newsletter carries a subject and single CTA, and the blog uses subheads. That is the job — one idea, every container.
